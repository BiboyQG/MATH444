\section*{Exercise 1}
Let $\sum a_n$ be a convergent series with positive terms. Prove that $\sum \sqrt{a_n a_{n+1}}$ is also convergent. \\

\textbf{Solution: } \\

For any two positive real numbers, we have:
\begin{align*}
\sqrt{a_n a_{n+1}} \leq \frac{a_n + a_{n+1}}{2}
\end{align*}

Therefore:
\begin{align*}
\sum_{n=1}^{N} \sqrt{a_n a_{n+1}} &\leq \sum_{n=1}^{N} \frac{a_n + a_{n+1}}{2} \\ 
&= \frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}(a_n + a_{n+1}) \\
&= \frac{1}{2}\left[(a_1 + a_2) + (a_2 + a_3) + \cdots + (a_N + a_{N+1})\right]\\
&= \frac{1}{2}\left[a_1 + 2a_2 + 2a_3 + \cdots + 2a_N + a_{N+1}\right]\\
&= \frac{a_1}{2} + \sum_{n=2}^{N}a_n + \frac{a_{N+1}}{2}
\end{align*}

As $N \to \infty$:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Since $\sum a_n$ converges, by theorem, $a_{N+1} \to 0$
    \item Let $S = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_n$, which is finite by convergence
\end{itemize}

Therefore:
\begin{align*}
\lim_{N \to \infty}\sum_{n=1}^{N} \sqrt{a_n a_{n+1}} &\leq \frac{a_1}{2} + \lim_{N \to \infty}\sum_{n=2}^{N}a_n + \frac{\lim_{N \to \infty}a_{N+1}}{2}\\
&= \frac{a_1}{2} + (S - a_1) + 0\\
&= \frac{a_1}{2} + S - a_1\\
&= S - \frac{a_1}{2}
\end{align*}

Since $S$ is finite and $a_1 > 0$, the series $\sum \sqrt{a_n a_{n+1}}$ is bounded above by a finite value. By theorem, we conclude that $\sum \sqrt{a_n a_{n+1}}$ is convergent.

\section*{Exercise 2}
Find the set of all cluster points of 
$$A := \left\{2 - \frac{1}{n^2} : n \in \mathbb{N}\right\}.$$
Justify your answer. \\

\textbf{Solution: } \\

\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
    \item For 2: \\
    Consider the sequence $(x_n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ where $x_n = 2 - \frac{1}{n^2}$ for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$.
    We have:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item $x_n \in A$ for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ (by construction)
        \item $x_n \neq 2$ for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ (since $\frac{1}{n^2} > 0$)
        \item $\lim x_n = \lim (2 - \frac{1}{n^2}) = 2 - 0 = 2$
    \end{itemize}

    By theorem, 2 is a cluster point of $A$.

    \item Next we show that any other point $c \in A$ with $c \neq 2$ is not a cluster point of $A$. \\
    
    Let $c \in A$, so $c = 2 - \frac{1}{k^2}$ for some $k \in \mathbb{N}$.

    For $c$ to be a cluster point, we need a sequence $(x_n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ with $x_n \in A$, $x_n \neq c$ for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, and $\lim x_n = c$.

    Since the elements of $A$ form a strictly increasing sequence converging to 2, no subsequence of elements from $A \setminus \{c\}$ can converge to $c$.

    \item Next we show that any other point $c \notin A$ with $c \neq 2$ is not a cluster point of $A$. \\

    Let $c \in A$, so $c = 2 - \frac{1}{k^2}$ for some $k \in \mathbb{N}$. \\

    For any such $c$, either:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item $c > 2$: All elements of $A$ are less than 2
        \item $c < 1$: All elements of $A$ are greater than or equal to 1
        \item $1 < c < 2$: Since $c \notin A$ and the elements of $A$ form a strictly increasing sequence, there must be consecutive elements $a_k < c < a_{k+1}$ in $A$, so no sequence of elements from $A$ can converge to $c$.
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

Therefore, the set of all cluster points of $A$ is $\{2\}$.

\section*{Exercise 3}
Show that 
$$\lim_{x \to 0} \cos \frac{x + 1}{x}$$
does not exist. \\

\textbf{Solution: } \\

According to Theorem, for $f: A \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ with $c$ as a cluster point of $A$, the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[(1)]
    \item $f$ has no limit at $c$.
    \item There exists a sequence $(x_n)$ with $x_n \in A$ and $x_n \neq c$ for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ such that
    $\lim x_n = c$ and $(f(x_n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ diverges.
\end{enumerate}

Let $f(x) = \cos\frac{x+1}{x}$ and $c = 0$, and let the sequence $x_n = \frac{1}{n\pi}$ for $n \in \mathbb{N}$. \\

We know that $\lim_{n\to\infty} x_n = 0$ since:
\begin{align*}
\lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{1}{n\pi} &= \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{1}{n} \cdot \frac{1}{\pi} \\
&= \frac{1}{\pi}\lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{1}{n} \\
&= 0
\end{align*}

For $f(x_n)$:
\begin{align*}
f(x_n) &= \cos\left(\frac{x_n+1}{x_n}\right)\\
&= \cos\left(\frac{\frac{1}{n\pi}+1}{\frac{1}{n\pi}}\right)\\
&= \cos(1 + n\pi) \\
&= (-1)^n\cos(1)
\end{align*}

Since $\cos(1) \neq 0$, the sequence $(f(x_n))$ alternates between the values $\cos(1)$ and $-\cos(1)$ as $n$ increases, which means it diverges. \\

Therefore, by theorem, $\lim_{x \to 0} \cos \frac{x + 1}{x}$ does not exist.
